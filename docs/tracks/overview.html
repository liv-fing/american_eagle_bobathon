About the Labs
There are four main labs below, each with a different purpose. Together they take you from understanding an application to modernizing it.
Read first
Here is what each main lab covers and why it matters. You will work through them in order, and each one builds on the one before it.
Lab 1 — Code Explanation & Architecture Documentation (~40 min)

You start by browsing the FLGHT4nn library and previewing the green-screen layouts in the Object Browser. Next you initialize Bob's context with /init and use the Understand dashboard to build a live dependency map of the programs, files, service programs, menus, and commands. Finally, you have Bob write an architecture explanation and an Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD). You finish with a documented application that Bob understands, and you'll use that context in the later labs.

💡 Why it matters: Before you change an application, you need to understand it. Bob does in minutes what normally takes days of reading source.
Lab 2 — Program-Level Explanation & Modernization (~15 min)

You open FRS409, a 30-year-old OPM RPG program, and ask Bob to explain what it does. Then you run Bob's RPG Modernization workflow to convert it to modern free-format ILE RPG. Bob compiles the result on your IBM i, and you review the modernization summary. The modernized source ends up in FLGHT4nn/QRPGLESRC/FRS409.RPGLE.

💡 Why it matters: Most IBM i shops have older programs that only a few people still understand. This lab shows how to make that code readable and maintainable.
Lab 3 — Ask Bob About Your System (~10 min)

You ask Bob three plain-English questions about your live IBM i. They cover which active jobs use the most CPU, what the top job is doing, and which programs in your library haven't been used recently. You don't need to know any system commands.

💡 Why it matters: Anyone on the team can get clear answers about the system without knowing the underlying commands.
Lab 4 — Generate a React Carbon App from a Green Screen (~30 min)

You open the 5250 green screen in Bob's built-in terminal and take a screenshot of it. You create a helper skill so Bob knows how to run React and Vite on IBM i PASE. Then Bob generates a modern React app styled with IBM Carbon from the screenshot. You forward a port and open the running app in your browser.

💡 Why it matters: It shows how to give an existing application a modern face without rewriting the business logic.
Labs 5 to 7 are additional labs you can try if time allows. You will find them in the Additional Labs group below Lab 4.
